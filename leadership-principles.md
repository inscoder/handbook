# Leadership Principles

**Leadership is an act, not a title.** It’s about leading the team to a better place. We view this as a responsibility, not a rank. Everyone at InsCoder is expected to lead, regardless of what it says on their title.

**These principles are hard-won.** They aren't slogans we found in a blog post. They are distilled from years of work, shared struggles, and lessons learned the hard way. They exist because they work.

**This list isn't etched in stone.** As we grow, we’ll learn new things and change our minds. But right now, these are the non-negotiable values we choose to live by.

## Customer Success First

Profit is the result of excellence, never the starting point. It is a byproduct of our customers' wins. When our customers achieve economic success, our own growth follows.

## Choosing Profit

We choose to make more than we spend. Profit grants us financial stability, the option to say "no," and the freedom to try things others would consider "too silly" to attempt.

## Radical Candor

Trust is built on direct, honest feedback. We challenge each other directly because we care about our work and each other’s success. Avoiding the hard truth isn't kindness-it's a missed opportunity to grow.

## The Sunshine Test

We act in private as we would in public. If we cannot confidently defend a decision or action in the open, the answer is "no."

## Disagree and Commit

We debate using facts and logic over salesmanship or showmanship. We optimize for the best idea to win. We choose the difficult-right over the easy-wrong, and we never stay put just because we lack a unanimous vote. Once a decision is made, we support it collectively regardless of individual agreement.

## Lead and Move

Make fast decisions when the risks are reversible and non-catastrophic. Most decisions here fall into this category. Be thoughtful, but do not wait for perfect information, broad consensus, or top-down direction.  

## Be Fair

We treat everyone-clients, partners, and staff-with radical fairness. Even when we have the upper hand, or a contract that favors our side, we ensure the person across from us feels they are being treated "fairly." Fair is the minimum; where we can, we choose to be generous. We build relationships that last longer than a single deal.
